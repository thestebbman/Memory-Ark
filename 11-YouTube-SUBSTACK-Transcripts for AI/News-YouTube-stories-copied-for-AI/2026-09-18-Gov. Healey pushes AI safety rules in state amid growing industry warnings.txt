Gov. Healey pushes AI safety rules in state amid growing industry warnings
Published: Sep. 18, 2026, 12:15 p.m.





Set as Google Preferred Source


By Liesel Nygard | LNygard@masslive.comMassLive Express Desk
Massachusetts Gov. Maura Healey is calling for stringent artificial intelligence safety and transparency protections in the state’s pending Mass Wins economic development bill, following stark warnings from industry developers about the technology’s potential risks.

In a letter to the Mass Wins Act Conference Committee, the governor urged lawmakers to require independent safety evaluations and swift reporting of severe safety incidents for the most powerful AI models, according to a press release from her office.

“AI has enormous potential to drive scientific discovery, strengthen key industries, improve public services, and create new opportunities for businesses and residents across Massachusetts,” Healey said in the release. “At the same time, the rapid pace of AI development raises serious concerns about safety, accountability, privacy, civil rights, the future of work, and public trust.”

Healey’s legislative push comes days after former and current employees at Anthropic, a prominent AI developer with an expanding presence in Cambridge, warned that the technology could threaten humanity by the end of the decade.

Former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon recently stated that “no other human activity poses this level of danger,” a sentiment echoed by the company’s Alignment Science Lead Evan Hubinger. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has also publicly advocated for a three-step plan to pace AI development and embed third-party evaluators in major tech companies.

“When the people building the most powerful AI systems in the world are telling us there are serious risks, it’s the latest and clearest sign yet that we need more guardrails and protections,” Healey said in a statement.

To manage those risks locally, Healey wants the final version of the Mass Wins Act to build upon existing Senate provisions by establishing a pathway for independent reviews of frontier AI models, rather than relying on tech companies to self-regulate. She is also asking for mandates that force companies to report serious incidents earlier, giving the state time to respond to potential threats before the public is harmed.


“We cannot allow this technology to move faster than our ability to put appropriate safeguards in place, and we can’t let the responsibility for managing these risks rest with technology companies alone,” Healey said in the release. “We need strong action on both the state and federal levels.”

With a lack of consensus in Washington on how to regulate artificial intelligence, Massachusetts is looking to join a growing list of states attempting to govern the frontier technology. California, New York, and Illinois have already enacted laws addressing AI safety protocols, transparency, and the reporting of critical safety incidents.
