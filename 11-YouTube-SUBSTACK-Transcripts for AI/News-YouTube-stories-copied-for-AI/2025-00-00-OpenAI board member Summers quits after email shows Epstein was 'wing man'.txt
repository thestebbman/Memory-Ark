Larry Summers is resigning from OpenAI’s board of directors after Congress published emails showing that the economist kept a close friendship with Jeffrey Epstein for at least a decade after the infamous financier pleaded guilty to soliciting a minor for prostitution.

Summers, a former Treasury secretary and president of Harvard, said in a statement from his office on Wednesday that he’d be quitting the OpenAI role. He had joined the San Francisco startup’s board in 2023, in a move to help stabilize the company after the dramatic ouster and then reinstatement of CEO Sam Altman late that year.

OpenAI’s board confirmed his resignation to the New York Times and said in a statement about Summers: “We appreciate his many contributions and the perspective he brought to the board.”

Summers had said Tuesday that he’d be stepping back from public commitments, citing his “misguided decision” to keep communicating with Epstein and writing: “I am deeply ashamed of my actions and recognize the pain they have caused.” 

The emails that sank Summers’ OpenAI role were released on Nov. 12 by the House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform — a 20,000-page dump from the estate of Epstein, who died in 2019. The two men were known to have a relationship, but the new emails reveal the breadth and longevity of their conversations.

A long series of emails circled one woman that Summers was apparently attempting to woo, despite him already being married. The men discussed Summers’ probability of “getting horizontal” with her, and he repeatedly forwarded her emails to Epstein to give him further context on the relationship, the Harvard Crimson reported earlier this week. Epstein wrote in one message to Summers, “I’m a pretty good wing man , no?” to which the economist replied, “You were good. . .Think for now I’m going nowhere with her except economics mentor.”
