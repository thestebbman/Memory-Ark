By Jeanette DeForge | jdeforge@repub.com
SPRINGFIELD — The City Council decided to bring a $100,000 grant recommended to help fund an affordable housing project back to the full board months after it sparked accusations of ethics violations against the city clerk.

If approved, the Bella Apartments redevelopment team will receive a $100,000 Community Preservation Act to help fund a $40 million project to convert the former Brightwood Elementary School into about 55 apartments.


The grant was one of 19 recommended by the Community Preservation Act committee that decides how about $3 million raised with a tax surcharge on all homeowners and businesses should be spent.

The others were approved in June, but when it came to vote on Bella Apartments, City Councilor Justin Hurst accused City Clerk Gladys Oyola-Lopez of having a conflict of interest and corruption because she also serves as chairperson of the project leader New North Citizens Council, which owns Bella.

The vote was put off amid Hurst accusing Oyola-Lopez of possible wrong-doing, and sent to subcommittee. Later, Oyola-Lopez said Hurst was retaliating against and harassing her because she witnessed and reported an alleged attempt to pay people $10 and a fish dinner by Hurst’s campaign when he was running for mayor three years ago.

“I’m mystified at what the controversy could be,” said Scott Foster, the New North Citizens Council lawyer said during Monday’s subcommittee meeting. “They have no financial interest, they have no economic interest. There is no money coming to them.”

Foster explained Oyola-Lopez became chairwoman of the board and Vanessa Otero was hired as executive director after director Maria Lingus was fired during a shakeup of the agency last year. In their new roles, they were also named managers of Bella Apartments.

“It is really so you can have a person to sign things,” he said. “As this episode has proven out, it is nothing but a big pain. It is a hassle.”

But Hurst said Oyola-Lopez should have, at the very least, disclosed her interest when the grant came to a vote, since the City Council appoints her and essentially serves as her boss.


“Is it a concern that we are approving money for our clerk?” he said.

Hurst said he is worried voting on the issue could put the City Council in legal jeopardy. He said he hoping for a state Ethics Commission ruling before the body votes on the grant on Monday.

Hurst initially requested a legal ruling on the ethics question from City Solicitor Stephen Buoniconti, who declined and referred Hurst to the state Ethics Commission.

“The investigation directly relates to alleged activity by you during an election, and the city clerk and her staff oversaw that election, there is an appearance that your request targets the City Clerk (and) could be viewed as an intimidation tactic against a witness in the case,” Buoniconti said to Hurst in the ruling. “The Law Department will not interfere in an ongoing investigation nor engage in any activity that may be perceived as intimidating a witness in an active criminal investigation.”

After that, Hurst proposed the City Council hire a private lawyer to look into the issue, but it was rejected in a 5-4 vote. Four members were absent during that July meeting.

During the Monday subcommittee meeting, several councilors questioned if there is a conflict of interest. City Councilor Zaida Govan, who also serves as head of the Indian Orchard Citizens Council, said her group has received Community Preservation Act grants to improve parks and restore a fire station without any controversy.


Councilor Melvin Edwards said he is the president of Maple High/Six Corners Neighborhood Council and has sat on the board of other non-profits, including Keep Springfield Beautiful, and both have received grants without concern.

“If there is an issue with ethics it should be stated, not just in theory but as fact,” Edwards said. “To this moment, I have yet to hear how A is connected to B.”

While Hurst argued Oyola-Lopez should have legally disclosed her role with the New North Citizens Council, Edwards disagreed, saying the law applies only to people who vote and the clerk serves as an administrative assistant.

“The clerk is not a decision-maker by definition,” Edwards said.

Oyola-Lopez also received a ruling from city lawyers who said she has no obligation or responsibility to disclose her involvement with New North Citizens Council, which has also been widely reported, her lawyer Zachary Vargo said.

Anthony Wilson, a former city clerk in Springfield who now works as a lawyer, backed up Edwards agreeing the disclosure law does not apply to the clerk.
