By Jim Kinney | jkinney@repub.com
SPRINGFIELD — In a world of constant cyberattacks, like the one that paralyzed city schools this month, the city’s water remains secure.

It’s protected by an air gap. In computer science parlance, that means the water treatment plant systems are not connected to the internet.

No one can control anything from off-site, said Springfield Water & Sewer Commission Executive Director Joshua D. Schimmel at a meeting Thursday.


The subject of cyberattacks came up in reference to the high-profile attack on Springfield’s schools.

Friday, Springfield Mayor Dominic J. Sarno said he is asking federal authorities for financial help dealing with the attack and strengthening the city’s defenses.


Sarno said the city is under constant threat — a sentiment echoed by Schimmel at the meeting.

“We get attacked constantly, every day,” Schimmel said.

The Commission is a member of the Water Information Sharing and Analysis Center, or WaterISAC. It is the communications and information-sharing organization for the water and wastewater industries.

WaterISAC gives daily updates.

“A week before you hear it on the news, we are already aware of it,” he said.

The Water Commission plans to meet with the city schools to go over vulnerabilities.
