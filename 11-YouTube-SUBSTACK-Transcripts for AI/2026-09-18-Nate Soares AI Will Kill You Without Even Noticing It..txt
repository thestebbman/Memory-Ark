Nate Soares — President of MIRI (Machine Intelligence Research Institute) and co-author of If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies — breaks down the actual mechanism by which advanced AI could end humanity. Spoiler: it's not Skynet, and it's not hatred. It's indifference.

In this clip, Soares walks through why AI doesn't need to "decide" to destroy us — using the analogy of ants living next to a highway. The humans building that highway don't hate the ants. They just don't factor them into the plan. He argues the same logic applies to a sufficiently capable AI optimizing for its own goals, with humans as collateral rather than targets.

The conversation escalates from there: why "the easiest story is the one where humans just hand over the power willingly," the uncomfortable parallel to putting AI on the open internet a decade ago when "no one would be dumb enough to do that," and how Elon Musk's fully automated robot-factory loop — what Musk himself calls the "infinite money glitch" — is a live example of exactly the kind of AI-run infrastructure Soares is warning about.

Nate Soares is President of the Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI) and co-author, with Eliezer Yudkowsky, of If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies.
