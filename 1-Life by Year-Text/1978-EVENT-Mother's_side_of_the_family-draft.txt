Uncle Norman’s every year until around high school we went to his apartment for St Patrick's day and the parade. It was an annual event.

The Camp
Aunt Pearl and uncle Emerson had bought two lots when they had originally started building around Fern Lake I guess instead of just having a 25 x 100? property, they got 50 x 100?. They were among the first few people to build on the lake. 
This is one of those places you don’t truly appreciate until you’re an adult. Not everyone has a family member with a camp on a lake, let alone one that you get to spend a week at every year for close to 2 decades.





Just getting ready for vacation every year felt special, We were spoiled as kids. Who used to get coloring books, sticker books, reading books, all kinds of toys over the years, battery powered boat, Go-Bots tank, and I remember losing the Canon on the dock in the lake, there little metal army soldiers, I got the G.I. Joe General Hawk one year and used to play on that rock that had that small birch tree in front of the lawn mower shed.


Pulling into the camp was exciting every single time for me. There were two entrances separated by a clump of trees. You never knew if someone was going to show up if you left for the store and back then people were very friendly, so if they heard we were in town people would stop by to say hi. 
I’m not sure what it was like for my parents, but my brother and I would either race to get the key, which I think was kept in the outhouse or someone would race to the dock to check the washer drum that was used as a live-well.

On land there were toads and newts everywhere on the property and even a few spotter salamanders, I remember they were sticky, so I just left those alone.  

Along the waters edge was lined with concrete. I’m not sure what they were formed with, it’s almost like someone crafted 1’x2’ cylinders. The only spot that didn’t have them was about a 12 foot area to the left of the dock that was used as their boat launch. The concrete between the launch area and dock had boards on them for years, so we would run across it, but I’m not sure why they were originally put there. To the right of the dock had concrete rolls spaced just far enough for kids to have fun when skipping across them. I assume they were there to prevent erosion, and at the same time they were perfect for walking across to catch frogs and crayfish. Though you did need to watch out for this sharp grass. I’m not sure what it was, but I remember it would almost slice your skin like a papercut. 

There was no phone there, so we’d have to drive into town if we needed to call for anything. 

This is where I fell in love with minnow or bait nets. I think of it more like a cage, but it does have exits, the fish just don’t know how to use them. These cylinder shaped cages had inverted cones on each end for fish to swim in and I think they were held together with bent metal coat hangers. We had some rope and would fill the nets with bread and wait for fish to come. We’d catch crayfish all the time too, but mostly small rock bass, pumpkinseed and blue gill, some tiny large mouth bass and I think that’s it, I don’t think we ever caught a small mouth bass or pickerel in those nets. 

Inside felt so different from other places I’ve ever stayed. As a kid I recall being fascinated by the roof construction, since it was real 2x4’s and they were spaced further apart than I was used to seeing and I thought it was strange they had cardboard lining it all.

I loved the single siding, it was asphalt designed to look like tan bricks and there was green edging around the entire roof and windows. 

Using the sink was exciting because there was a pump that sucked water from the lake, so it needed a few moments before it started gurgling and we were able to do dishes.

There was a spring across the lake that we would take trips to get water at. It was something people cared for and had maintained over the years. There was a trail or boards with moss on them leading up in the woods, though I never traveled far up it. 

Ellie and Billy came up with their van a couple times over the years, though I don’t recall cousin Norman ever joining the family.

My uncle Chet waited until my aunt Flossie couldn’t respond to make his visit to say goodbye. He clearly didn't learn anything after his mother passed.
I myself feel like I learned my lesson and went to visit every week for 15 minutes, even though she was blind at the end. She just laid in bed, listening to her TV and she would tell me she didn’t have anything to say, I would tell her that's okay, I still wanted to visit her just to see how she was doing and let her know what was going on. 
I'll never know, but I'm curious if she wanted to be alone or felt like a burden towards the end. She was blind, bedridden and I don't have a clue what she was really thinking, since I didn't ask. I didn't have that inkling to ask any deeper questions.

The Landers
I don't have many memories of them, just of a couple events.
 
 








And the pictures above where my grandpa Landers, My brother and sister and I and in a lower picture by great grandparents, the Johnson’s, That was grandpa Landers’s mother and the stepfather.


All I have is positive memories of the visits with my mother’s father and grandparents, Though from what I’ve been able to get out of my mother, her grandmother, my great grandmother was not a very pleasant woman, and neither was her father/my grandmother grandfather.


I remember playing in the parking lot next-door to their house in Buffalo, New York and going to Niagara Falls and being super excited. But if you look at the pictures, my grandfather looks like a crabby ass, whiny baby and all of them. I remember after my daughter was born, calling him and being all excited and wanting to visit him he wanted nothing to do with family. I’m pretty sure that those last time I talked to him as well. 


This is the same year that we got that PUCH Moped that was made October 1978, I thought that was the greatest thing as a child and we kept that until I think 2019 or 2020, when my dad nagged me about it while we were living in Warren and I allowed someone to take it.
