Homo Erectus our early ancestors lived up to 1.8 million years ago until about 400,000 

years ago, their remains have been found in several locations, they left evidence of the

first controlled use of fire, they left evidence of their ability to create tools as well as 

indications of an ability to communicate and plan ahead. 


The first remains of Homo Erectus were first discovered in Trinil by Eugene Dubois in

the late 19th century. Since then remains have been found as far north as Boxgrove, 

England dating 500,000 years ago, as far south as Swartkrans, South Africa dating 1.5 

million years ago , as far north east as Zhoukoudia, China dating 500,000 years ago

and as far south east as Mojokerto, Indonesia dating 1.8 million years ago. 


Evidence of Homo Erectus’s ability to use fire has been found in Swarkran in South

Africa in deposits as early as 1.3 million years old, Bones found here show signs of being

heated far beyond what Archeologists expect to find from naturally occuring fires. There 

are also remains of a roughly circular arrangement of fire crack basalt cobble with 

artifacts and animals bones in a Kao Poh Nam rock shelter in Thailand dating Back 

700,000 years. Most consider it unlikely that Homo Erectus could have survived the

lower temperatures of Northern China, the mountain Highlands of Central Asia and most 

of Europe.


The tools created by Homo Erectus who live in Africa, Europe and southwestern Asia are 

considered part of the Acheulean Tool Tradition. Their most notable piece of this 

tradition is the hand-axe made from naturally occurring material that flaked when struck 

correctly and the crafter was able to create the desired shape. This group also used 

cleavers, picks and knives and also flake tools made from the byproducts of the hand-axe 

and cleaver. Although common in in Africa, Europe and southwest Asia, stone tools are 

not common in southeastern Asia. Here it is likely that the tools were made from bamboo

and other local woods that do not preserve well, but make excellent knives and scrapers. 


Though it is unknown whether Homo Erectus had linguistic abilities, the need to plan 

ahead for seasonal changes, As well as a need to communicate hunting strategies indicate 

some form as ability to transfer information. 



Although current archeological evidence of when Homo Erectus lived, the locations they

traveled to, their abilities to use fire and create tools has provided insight into our early 

ancestors lives. New evidence is still being discovered furthering our understanding of the 

lives, culture and abilities of early ancestors.
